WDR shelf

The World Development Report is the World Bank's annual report on one big question of development; the 2008 edition was the thirtieth. Here are the twenty-six from 2000/01 to 2026, with the 1999/2000 edition for context.

Each card gives the report's main argument in a few sentences, paraphrased from the summary the World Bank publishes with it, and links to the report's record in the World Bank's document library and to any chapter of this book that draws on it. Titles are as listed on the World Bank's WDR archive page.1

How the subjects moved

The reports

WDR 1999/2000

Entering the 21st Century

Two forces would shape development in the new century: the integration of the world economy and growing demands for self-government. The report called them globalisation and localisation, and argued that states would have to strike a balance between international and local partners.

Growth, trade and financeInstitutions and governance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2000/01

Attacking Poverty

Poverty is more than low income: it also means powerlessness, having no voice, vulnerability and fear. The report proposed attacking it on three fronts — opportunity, empowerment and security — through action at global, national and local levels.

Poverty and equity

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Development

WDR 2002

Building Institutions for Markets

Markets need institutions that carry information, enforce property rights and contracts, and keep competition alive. These should suit local conditions and be built by experiment, helped by open trade and open flows of information.

Institutions and governanceGrowth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2003

Sustainable Development in a Dynamic World

With three billion more people expected over fifty years, social and environmental strains could derail development unless policies and institutions improved. Many good policies were already known but went unused because of who would win and who would lose from them.

Environment and riskPoverty and equity

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2004

Making Services Work for Poor People

Health, schooling, water, sanitation and electricity too often fail poor people, both in access and in quality. The answer is stronger accountability — changing how services are delivered, how the public sector works and how aid is given — not only more money.

People: health, schooling, workInstitutions and governance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2005

A Better Investment Climate for Everyone

Firms of every size drive growth, jobs and tax revenue, and how much they invest depends on the climate governments create: property rights, regulation and tax, infrastructure, finance and labour markets, and corruption. Improving it should be a top priority.

Growth, trade and financeInstitutions and governance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2006

Equity and Development

Unequal opportunities reinforce one another in “inequality traps” and waste talent. Levelling the political and economic playing field, at home and in global markets, supports long-run development and the fight against poverty.

Poverty and equity

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Development

WDR 2007

Development and the Next Generation

The 1.3 billion people aged 12 to 24 were the largest youth cohort in history, and healthier and better educated than any before. Countries should invest in five transitions — learning, working, health, forming families and citizenship — before population ageing closes the window.

People: health, schooling, work

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Demography and labour

WDR 2008

Agriculture for Development

Three in four poor people in developing countries lived in rural areas. Farming was a vital tool for halving poverty and hunger: through smallholder productivity in Africa, rural jobs in Asia, and better care of land and water everywhere.

Poverty and equityGrowth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2009

Reshaping Economic Geography

As economies grow, production crowds into cities, coasts and well-connected countries. Growth will be unbalanced, but living standards can still converge, and the way to get both is economic integration.

Growth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2010

Development and Climate Change

Growth and poverty reduction remained the priority, but climate change makes both harder. Rich countries must cut their emissions; developing countries need more energy, transport and cities without the old carbon intensity, and must adapt to warming that is unlikely to be held below 2°C.

Environment and risk

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Energy, commodities and climate

WDR 2011

Conflict, Security, and Development

Violence becomes more likely when institutions fail to protect citizens, curb corruption, give access to justice or create jobs. Legitimate institutions are the key to stability, and outside partners must act faster and stay longer.

Institutions and governance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2012

Gender Equality and Development

Gender equality is a goal in its own right and also “smart economics”. Growth alone does not close every gap, so policy should target female mortality and education, economic opportunity, women's voice, and the passing of inequality from one generation to the next.

People: health, schooling, workPoverty and equity

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Demography and labour

WDR 2013

Jobs

Jobs are the cornerstone of development: they cut poverty, raise productivity and hold societies together. Governments should get the fundamentals right, design labour policy well, and find and unblock the jobs that would do most for development in their country.

People: health, schooling, workGrowth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Demography and labour

WDR 2014

Risk and Opportunity

After financial crises, disasters and epidemics, the report urged people and institutions to stop fighting crises and start managing risk in advance. Many developing countries came through the 2008 crisis well because of reforms made before it.

Environment and riskGrowth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2015

Mind, Society, and Behavior

People decide by intuition, through the lens of their culture and under social pressure, not as textbook optimisers. Policies built on how people actually choose — defaults, social rewards, framing — can be cheap and effective, and poverty itself shapes decisions.

People: health, schooling, workInstitutions and governance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2016

Digital Dividends

Internet access and mobile phones were spreading fast, even among the poorest households, but the wider benefits lagged. They would come only with competition in telecoms, a good business climate, education and health, and good governance.

Technology and data

WDR 2017

Governance and the Law

Why do sound policies so often fail? Their success depends on commitment, coordination and cooperation, which imbalances of power can undermine, and laws on paper differ from how they are applied. Bad governance can persist, but change is possible.

Institutions and governance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2018

Learning to Realize Education's Promise

Getting children into school is not enough: in many countries they learn little there, and the disadvantaged learn least. The causes lie in whole education systems, not only in classrooms.

People: health, schooling, work

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Development

WDR 2019

The Changing Nature of Work

Fears that robots would take people's jobs looked, on balance, unfounded. Technology changes the skills employers want and the terms on which people work, so governments should invest in people and extend social protection to all workers, paid for with broader taxes.

Technology and dataPeople: health, schooling, work

WDR 2020

Trading for Development in the Age of Global Value Chains

Global value chains powered the trade boom after 1990, made up almost half of world trade and helped poor countries catch up, but stalled after 2008. They can still lift growth if developing countries reform, rich countries stay open and multilateral cooperation revives.

Growth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Trade

WDR 2021

Data for Better Lives

Data can improve poor people's lives but can also harm them. The report called for a new social contract on data: using and reusing data to create value, sharing that value fairly, and building trust that data will not be misused.

Technology and dataInstitutions and governance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2022

Finance for an Equitable Recovery

After COVID-19, hidden financial risks — bad loans and unsustainable debts — threatened the recovery as support was withdrawn. The report urged transparent handling of bad loans, insolvency reform, new ways of lending, and better management of government debt.

Growth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2023

Migrants, Refugees, and Societies

About 184 million people live outside their country of nationality. The report's “Match and Motive Matrix” sorts movements by how well migrants' skills fit the destination's needs and why they moved, so policy can differ for each kind.

People: health, schooling, work

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Demography and labour · Sub-Saharan Africa

WDR 2024

The Middle-Income Trap

Since the 1970s, income per person in the median middle-income country has stayed below a tenth of the US level. To escape, countries need two shifts: from investment to absorbing technology from abroad (“infusion”), and then to innovation.

Growth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank) · In this book: Development

WDR 2025

Standards for Development

Standards for measurement, compatibility and quality are invisible infrastructure for trade and technology. Countries should adapt, align and then author standards as they develop, build the institutions that test and certify, and avoid overregulation.

Institutions and governanceGrowth, trade and finance

Report record (World Bank)

WDR 2026

The Promise of Artificial Intelligence

Few developing countries can push the AI frontier soon, but adapting available AI to local problems — health, farming, small firms, public services — could achieve in a decade what might otherwise take a century. That needs electricity, connectivity, skills, computing and local-language data, and neither hype nor hysteria.

Technology and data
