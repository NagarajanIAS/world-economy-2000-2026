About this book

How the book was built, how to read its charts, and what is not yet confirmed.

Authorship and licence

This is an independent study of the world economy from 2000 to 2026. The author set its scope, structure and standards of evidence, and directed Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, to do the work: reading the reports, drafting the chapters, building the data and charts, and checking the figures against the source documents. It is not an official publication of any government or international organisation, and its analysis and judgements are not theirs.

Method

The narrative follows what the institutions that watch the world economy said, as they said it, and sets that against the data as we now have it. For each phase the book reads the IMF World Economic Outlook editions published during it (executive summary and Chapter 1, plus analytical chapters where relevant), the Global Financial Stability Report from 2002 and the Fiscal Monitor from 2009, and the World Development Reports published in the period. Other sources (central bank records, official inquiries, academic papers) come in where the reports are silent or where a question is contested.

Abbreviations are spelled out the first time they appear in each chapter, and the Glossary lists them all. Each chapter's table of reports ends with a key to the abbreviations it uses.

Every chapter has a section on India: how the phase, theme or economy in question touched India, drawing on India's own official reports, above all the Economic Survey and the Reserve Bank's publications.

Nothing is taken from memory. Report titles, dates, figures and quotations were read from the documents themselves, and each is footnoted to its edition, chapter and page. Anything that could not be checked carries a visible unverified marker and is listed below. Quotations are kept short; the rest is paraphrase.

Data and vintages

Cross-country figures come from the IMF World Economic Outlook database, October 2026 edition, unless a chart says otherwise. IMF data are revised with every edition, and the October 2026 figures for past years can differ from what the IMF published at the time. Chapters that compare forecasts with outcomes say which vintage each number comes from. Other series come from the World Bank's World Development Indicators, FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis), the ECB Data Portal, the BIS, and Robert Shiller's long-run US stock market data. The sources page lists every series with its code, coverage and retrieval date.

Estimates and projections. In the IMF data, years after a country's last actual year are staff estimates or projections. For most economies in the October 2026 database that means 2026 onward, and for some it includes 2025. Charts draw these years with a dotted line on a shaded background, and tooltips label them. The text treats 2025–26 figures as provisional.

How to read the charts

  • Phase bands. Alternating light bands mark the book's nine phases, numbered at the top. They can be switched off with the “Phases & recessions” box.
  • Recession shading. On world charts, darker grey marks years in which world real GDP per person fell (World Bank WDI, NY.GDP.PCAP.KD): 1991, 2009 and 2020. On US charts it marks NBER recession months (FRED USREC). The book uses the per-person rule because it can be computed from the data; it is not an official definition.
  • Tools. Compare countries draws any of 47 IMF and World Bank indicators for up to eight economies; the world map shades every country for one year; the WDR shelf summarises each World Development Report.
  • Controls. Every chart has a year range, a data table and a CSV download. Click a legend entry to hide or show a series. Some charts let you add and remove economies.
  • Colours. Each economy keeps the same colour throughout the book (for example the United States is always blue and China always red). Aggregates such as “advanced economies” use dashed lines. The palette was checked for colour-vision deficiency, and lines also differ by dash pattern.

The nine phases

The phases follow turning points in the world economy, not calendar years. Phase 2 ends and Phase 3 begins in August 2007, when the money markets seized. Phase 5 begins in mid-2014 with the collapse in oil prices. Phase 9 covers 2026, which is still in progress. Its account will change as data and reports arrive.

Not yet confirmed

These items are flagged in the text where they appear, or kept out of it until confirmed. The working list is in NOTES.md.

  • The United Nations' final Millennium Development Goals report (2015) could not be fetched. The Development chapter marks the one place where it would be cited.
  • The World Bank's definition of a global recession (in the June 2020 Global Economic Prospects). The book uses its own data-based rule instead.
  • The printed page numbers of one section of the Hennessey, Holtz-Eakin and Thomas dissent in the FCIC report, which did not survive text extraction. The footnote cites PDF pages.
  • Two timeline entries (China's WTO accession and the 2002 euro cash changeover) rest on short official pages rather than full documents.
  • The 2026 war in the Middle East. The IMF reports read for Phase 9 do not name the parties or say who began it; only the Indian finance ministry's February 2026 Monthly Economic Review does, and the chapter attributes it there. No source read gives the dates of the ceasefire or later escalations, so none are given.
  • On the WDR shelf, 14 of the 27 links to the World Bank's document library could not be tested, because the site began refusing requests during the check. The other 13 worked.
  • Shiller's monthly S&P data end in September 2023. Later months are FRED's S&P 500 series, spliced at that date.

Using the book offline

The book is a set of static pages. Open index.html in a browser; no internet connection or server is needed. All libraries (ECharts for charts, MiniSearch for search) are included in assets/vendor/. Press / to search, and [ and ] to move between chapters. Pages print cleanly, with charts and source lines.
