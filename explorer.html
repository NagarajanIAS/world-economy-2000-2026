How to use it

  • Economies. Use Add to bring in up to eight economies or groups, and × to remove one. Each economy keeps the colour it has elsewhere in the book.
  • Years. The From and to boxes set the range; you can also drag the slider under the chart. The chart opens at 1990 or the first year of data, whichever is later.
  • Numbers. Data table shows the values behind the lines and CSV saves them, with the source line.
  • Bookmarks. The page address records the indicator and the economies, so a view can be saved or shared.

The data

There are 47 indicators. Those marked IMF come from the World Economic Outlook database, October 2026; those marked World Bank come from the World Development Indicators, retrieved in November 2026. Each indicator's name here is shortened; the source's own label is printed above the chart. Groups follow each source's own definitions: the IMF's Sub-Saharan Africa, for example, has 45 economies and the World Bank's has 48. When you switch between an IMF and a World Bank indicator, the page swaps the world, euro area and Sub-Saharan Africa groups for the other source's. The sources page lists every series with its retrieval date.

Two cautions. IMF figures for 2026 onward, and for some economies 2025, are estimates or projections; the chart shades them. And World Bank series stop at different years for different countries, so the latest point on a line is not always the same year.
