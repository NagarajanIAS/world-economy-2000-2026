# Reading notes: IMF WEO October 2014 – October 2019 (read 22 Nov 2026)

Method as in IMF_2003-2006.md: PDFs fetched inside an imf.org page in the built-in browser and decoded there.
Page numbers come from the printed running heads; "p. ?" where no running head was readable.
Quotes are under 15 words; everything else is paraphrase.
URL patterns: 2014–2016 editions under .../imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/YYYY/0N/pdf/ (_exesumpdf.pdf, _c1pdf.pdf, _textpdf.pdf);
2019 editions under https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2019/{april,october}/english/ (execsum.pdf, ch1.pdf).

## WEO October 2014 — *Legacies, Clouds, Uncertainties* (Executive Summary pp. xv–xvi)
- World growth 2014 marked down to 3.3% (0.4 point below April); 2015 projection 3.8%.
- Medium-term risks: "secular stagnation and low potential growth" in advanced economies (p. xvi); decline in potential growth in emerging markets.
- Public infrastructure investment could support demand now and potential output later.

## WEO April 2015 — *Uneven Growth: Short- and Long-Term Factors* (Executive Summary pp. xv–xvi; Ch. 1 p. 5)
- World growth projected 3.5% in 2015. Oil exporters face a large terms-of-trade shock; lower oil prices are an opportunity to reform energy subsidies and taxation.
- Welcomes the ECB's expansion of asset purchases to sovereign bonds. A further sharp dollar appreciation could cause financial tensions in emerging markets.
- Ch. 1 p. 5: the oil price fall was driven mainly by supply: rising non-OPEC output, especially in the United States; faster recovery in some stressed OPEC producers (Iraq); and especially OPEC's November 2014 decision to keep production unchanged despite falling prices.

## WEO October 2015 — *Adjusting to Lower Commodity Prices* (Executive Summary pp. xv–xvii; Ch. 1 pp. 7, 17)
- World growth 2015 projected 3.1%, 0.3 point below 2014. Emerging-market growth projected to fall for the fifth year in a row.
- China's slowdown in line with forecasts, but its cross-border repercussions "greater than previously envisaged" (weaker metals prices, lower exports to China).
- Lift-off of US policy rates likely to tighten external financing conditions.
- Ch. 1 p. 7: renminbi depreciated about 4% against the dollar after the announced increase in exchange-rate flexibility, yet remained some 10% above its 2014 average in real effective terms.
- Ch. 1 p. 17: India's inflation expected to decline further in 2015 on lower oil and farm prices.

## WEO April 2016 — *Too Slow for Too Long* (Executive Summary p. xv; Ch. 1 pp. 6–7, 19)
- World growth 2016 projected 3.2%, 0.2 point below January.
- Ch. 1 p. 6: market turmoil early in 2016 triggered by concerns about policy space in advanced economies, very low oil prices, and the speed of China's slowdown; markets rallied after mid-February.
- Ch. 1 p. 7: in late January the Bank of Japan introduced a negative interest rate on marginal excess reserves; the ECB eased further in March (more asset purchases, corporate bonds, new long-term loans, lower rates).

## WEO October 2016 — *Subdued Demand: Symptoms and Remedies*
- Foreword by Maurice Obstfeld (Economic Counsellor), p. xiii: "the unexpected vote for Brexit on June 23" left the UK's future trade and financial relations with the other 27 EU members unclear.
- Executive Summary p. xv: world growth 3.1% in 2016, 3.4% in 2017. The Brexit vote and the US election campaign highlighted "a fraying consensus" about cross-border economic integration; protectionist approaches gaining appeal. Emerging Asia and "especially India" resilient.
- Ch. 1 p. 8: a large stock of advanced-economy sovereign bonds trading at negative yields (with reference to the Oct 2016 GFSR).
- **Ch. 2, "Global Trade: What's Behind the Slowdown?", p. 63**: trade growth slowed since 2012 relative to its history and to GDP. Weak economic activity, in particular investment, accounts for "up to three-fourths of the slowdown". The waning pace of trade liberalisation and a recent uptick in protectionism also hold trade back (limited quantitative impact so far); slower growth of global value chains also played an important part.
- Ch. 1 p. 27: scenario box on protectionism: a generalised rise in tariff and non-tariff barriers causes a collapse in trade and a sharp fall in global output.

## WEO April 2019 — *Growth Slowdown, Precarious Recovery* (Executive Summary; Ch. 1 p. 7)
- After strong growth in 2017 and early 2018, global activity slowed notably in the second half of 2018. China's growth declined with regulatory tightening of shadow banking and higher trade tensions with the United States; the euro area lost momentum.
- Ch. 1 p. 7: global growth to moderate from 3.6% in 2018 to 3.3% in 2019, 0.4 point below October 2018.
- Risks: further escalation of trade tensions; a no-deal Brexit.

## WEO October 2019 — *Global Manufacturing Downturn, Rising Trade Barriers* (Executive Summary pp. xvi–xvii; Ch. 1)
- Exec. Summary: manufacturing momentum weakened to levels not seen since the global financial crisis; rising trade and geopolitical tensions raised uncertainty about the trading system. In China, debt-reining regulation and trade tensions took a toll.
- Ch. 1 p. 2: global trade volume in H1 2019 just 1% above a year earlier, the slowest six-month pace since 2012.
- Ch. 1 p. 6: sovereign bond yields fell sharply from March to late September 2019, some "deep into negative territory"; ECB cut its deposit rate and resumed QE.
- Ch. 1 p. 7: projected 2019 growth of 3.0% "is the weakest since 2009".
- Ch. 1 p. 9: severe distress in Argentina, Iran, Turkey and Venezuela accounts for about half the fall in world growth from 3.8% (2017) to 3.0% (2019).
- Ch. 1 p. 13: India to grow 6.1% in 2019 (1.2 points below April), held back by autos, real estate and uncertainty about non-bank financial companies; 7% in 2020.
- Ch. 1 Box (p. 33): US–China tariffs (implemented and announced by Aug 2019, with confidence, market and productivity effects) put global output about 0.8% below baseline at the 2020 trough; China's output falls 2% short term; US 0.6%.
