# Reading notes: IMF WEO April 2010 – April 2014 (read 22 Nov 2026)

Method as in IMF_2003-2006.md: each PDF was fetched inside an imf.org page in the built-in browser and
its text streams decoded there. Page numbers come from the printed running heads. Where a passage's
page is inferred from the order of running heads in the decoded text, it is marked "(by head order)".
Quotes are under 15 words; everything else is paraphrase.
PDF base: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/YYYY/0N/pdf/
Files read: _exesumpdf.pdf (executive summaries), and _c1pdf.pdf / _textpdf.pdf for Oct 2012 and Oct 2013.

## WEO April 2010 — *Rebalancing Growth*  (Executive Summary pp. xiv–xv)
- World output projected to rise about 4¼% in 2010, after contracting about ½% in 2009.
- Recovery described as "multispeed": weaker in advanced economies, stronger in emerging economies.
- Risk: concerns about Greece "could turn into a full-blown and contagious sovereign debt crisis".
- Credible medium-term fiscal consolidation strategies "urgently needed" in advanced economies.

## WEO October 2010 — *Recovery, Risk, and Rebalancing*  (Executive Summary pp. xv–xvii)
- Global growth projected 4.8% in 2010 and 4.2% in 2011; emerging and developing 7.1 / 6.4; advanced 2.7 / 2.2.
- "Fiscal adjustment needs to start in 2011" in most advanced economies.
- Chapter 3: fiscal consolidation "typically detracts from short-term growth".

## WEO April 2011 — *Tensions from the Two-Speed Recovery*  (Executive Summary pp. xvii–xviii)
- Two-speed recovery; overheating risks in many emerging economies.
- Capital-flow measures "can play a role" but are "not a substitute for macroeconomic tightening".
- Euro area: needs adequate funding for countries under market pressure, plus rigorous bank stress tests.

## WEO September 2011 — *Slowing Growth, Rising Risks*

## WEO April 2012 — *Growth Resumes, Dangers Remain*  (Executive Summary pp. xv–xvi)
- p. xv: after "a major setback during 2011", prospects gradually strengthening; recent improvements "very fragile".
- p. xv: without further action, fiscal policy could tighten very abruptly in the United States in 2013.
- p. xvi: a further escalation of the euro crisis could lower global output 2% and euro area output 3½% over two years relative to projections.
- p. xvi: welcomes combining the ESM and EFSF; LTROs and government-bond purchases should continue; banks must be recapitalised.
- p. xvi: countries with fiscal room "can reconsider the pace of consolidation"; others should let automatic stabilisers operate.

## WEO October 2012 — *Coping with High Debt and Sluggish Growth*
- Executive Summary pp. xvii–xviii: euro area needs a banking union and steps to fiscal integration; US legislators must remove the threat of the "fiscal cliff" and raise the debt ceiling, else the US could fall back into recession. Plans should target structural balances rather than nominal targets.
- Ch. 1 (text, same issue): "the evidence increasingly suggests that ... the fiscal multipliers are large" (Foreword, in the full-text PDF _textpdf.pdf; page not pinned).
- **Box 1.1, pp. 41–43, "Are We Underestimating Short-Term Fiscal Multipliers?"** — authors Olivier Blanchard and Daniel Leigh.
  - Regresses growth forecast errors for 2010–11 on planned fiscal consolidation in the April 2010 WEO, 28 economies (G20 advanced and EU).
  - Planned consolidation of 1% of GDP associated with a growth forecast error of about 1 percentage point: multipliers underlying the forecasts were too low by about 1 (range 0.4 to 1.2 across forecast sources and specifications).
  - Forecasters implicitly assumed multipliers of about 0.5; actual multipliers since the Great Recession "in the 0.9 to 1.7 range".
  - Attributed to substantial slack, monetary policy at the zero lower bound and synchronised adjustment across countries.
- Ch. 1 p. 21 (by head order): earlier WEO research found multipliers close to 1 when many countries adjust together; "may recently have been larger than 1".

## WEO April 2013 — *Hopes, Realities, Risks*  (Executive Summary pp. xv–xvi)
- Near-term risks "more symmetric": European and US actions addressed some of the gravest short-term risks.
- Euro area dangers: adjustment fatigue, weak balance sheets, broken credit channels in the periphery.
- US: failure to replace the sequester would lower growth; failure to raise the debt ceiling of much greater concern.
- Fiscal adjustment "needs to progress gradually"; for Japan, risks from renewed stimulus amid very high debt.
- Emerging economies: some policy tightening appropriate over the medium term.

## WEO October 2013 — *Transitions and Tensions*
- Executive Summary p. xv: global growth "in low gear"; China and many emerging economies coming off cyclical peaks. The end of US quantitative easing could bring a greater and longer-lasting tightening of global financial conditions than expected; some emerging economies "could even face severe balance of payments disruptions". Emerging economies should let exchange rates respond to fundamentals, using intervention to smooth excessive volatility.
- Ch. 1 p. 7 (by head order): after the Fed's midyear meetings and communication "hinting at tapering of asset purchases", markets raised expected policy rates; the Fed then decided not to begin tapering in September.
- Ch. 1 p. 16 (by head order): Fed statements about tapering caused "a surprisingly large tightening" of US monetary conditions; the jump in emerging-market local bond yields was roughly three times that in the April 2013 tightening scenario.
- Ch. 1 p. 5 (by head order): India's growth projections "marked down significantly", to 3¾% in 2013 and about 5% in 2014 (fiscal-year basis). China's forecast reduced to about 7½% for 2013–14.
- Ch. 1 figure (p. 10 by head order): Brazilian real, Indian rupee and South African rand depreciated by 8–16% against the dollar (figure covers 1 Jan / 22 May to 23 Sep 2013).
- Ch. 1 Table 1.1 (p. 2): India 2012 3.2, proj. 2013 3.8, 2014 5.1. (Table values; the text's 3¾ matches 3.8.)

## WEO April 2014 — *Recovery Strengthens, Remains Uneven*  (Executive Summary pp. xv–xvi)
- Global activity broadly strengthened, impetus mostly from advanced economies; their inflation undershot projections.
- Emerging economies disappointed in a less favourable external financial environment, yet still contributed "more than two-thirds of global growth".
- Euro area: risk that low inflation drifts toward deflation; more monetary easing, including unconventional measures, needed. Banking union needs a Single Resolution Mechanism and common backstops.
- Japan: implement the remaining two "arrows" of Abenomics.
- Emerging economies: let exchange rates adjust; use FX intervention to smooth volatility where reserves are adequate; tighten further where inflation or pass-through risk is high.

## India threads for Phase 4 (from the above)
- Oct 2013: India's 2013 growth projection marked down to 3¾%; the rupee among the currencies hit hardest after the Fed's May 2013 taper signals.
- Apr 2011: capital-flow measures as a complement to, not a substitute for, macro tightening (relevant to India's 2013 measures).
