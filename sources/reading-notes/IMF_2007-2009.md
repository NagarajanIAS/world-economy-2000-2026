# Reading notes — IMF WEO 2007–2008 (read in the browser pane, 22 Nov 2026)

Method: imf.org blocks scripted downloads; PDFs were read on screen in the built-in browser.
Page numbers are the printed roman numerals of the Executive Summary.

## WEO April 2007 — *Spillovers and Cycles in the Global Economy*
URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/2007/01/pdf/_exesumpdf.pdf
- p. xv: world economy "still looks well set for continued robust growth in 2007 and 2008" (quote, 13 words).
- p. xv: world grew 5.4% in 2006; growth expected to moderate to 4.9% in 2007 and 2008.
- p. xv: risks "less threatening than six months ago" but tilted to downside, with concerns increasing about financial risks; Feb–Mar 2007 volatility and US subprime concerns noted, but equity markets near highs.
- p. xv: US current account deficit 6½% of GDP in 2006.
- p. xvi: US growth expected 2.2% in 2007 from 3.3% in 2006; spillovers from US typically muted in a midcycle slowdown; US shock seen as US-specific.
- Ch. 4 "Decoupling the Train? Spillovers and Cycles in the Global Economy"; Ch. 3 "Exchange Rates and the Adjustment of External Imbalances"; Box 2.1 "Housing Market Slowdowns" (from the contents page).

## WEO October 2007 — *Globalization and Inequality*
URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/2007/02/pdf/_exesumpdf.pdf
- p. xiv: turbulence in financial markets has clouded prospects; 2008 global growth projection cut by almost ½ pp vs July 2007 Update, to "a solid 4¾ percent"; risks "firmly on the downside", centred on financial strains deepening.
- p. xiv: global growth above 5% in H1 2007; China 11½%; China, India, Russia = one-half of global growth over the past year.
- p. xv: Fed cut fed funds rate by 50 bp in September 2007; central banks injected liquidity in August.

## WEO April 2008 — *Housing and the Business Cycle*
URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/2008/01/pdf/_exesumpdf.pdf
- p. xiv: global expansion "losing speed in the face of a major financial crisis"; evidence of a broad credit squeeze "although not yet a full-blown credit crunch".
- p. xiv: commodity run-up partly due to financial factors (commodities as an asset class).
- p. xv: US current account deficit below 5% of GDP by Q4 2007.
- p. xv: global growth projected to slow to 3.7% in 2008 (½ pp below Jan 2008 Update; 1¼ pp below 2007) and to remain broadly unchanged in 2009; US to tip into a mild recession in 2008, modest recovery in 2009.
- p. xv: staff see a 25 percent chance that global growth drops to 3% or less in 2008 and 2009, "equivalent to a global recession" (IMF's then threshold).
- p. xv: greatest risk from deep losses on structured credit tied to US subprime.
- Ch. 1 p. 2, Table 1.1 (read on screen): world output 2007 4.9; projections 2008 3.7, **2009 3.8**; advanced economies 2009 1.3; US 2008 0.5, 2009 0.6; EMDEs 2009 6.6; world trade volume 2007 6.8, 2008 5.6, 2009 5.8.
  URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/2008/01/pdf/_c1pdf.pdf

## WEO October 2008 — *Financial Stress, Downturns, and Recoveries*
URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/2008/02/pdf/_exesumpdf.pdf
- p. xv: world economy "entering a major downturn in the face of the most dangerous financial shock in mature financial markets since the 1930s" (quote used: "the most dangerous financial shock in mature financial markets since the 1930s", 13 words).
- p. xv: crisis entered a tumultuous new phase in September; emergency resolutions of major US and European institutions.
- p. xv: "In hindsight", lax macroeconomic and regulatory policies may have let the global economy exceed its speed limit and contributed to imbalances in financial, housing and commodity markets.
- p. xvi: global growth 5.0% in 2007 → 3.9% in 2008 → **3.0% in 2009**, slowest since 2002; advanced economies in or close to recession H2 2008/early 2009.
- p. xvi: Ch. 4 finds that banking-rooted financial stress typically has more adverse effects than stress in equity or FX markets.

## WEO Update November 2008 — *Rapidly Weakening Prospects Call for New Policy Stimulus*
URL: https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/weo/2008/update/03/index.htm (HTML)
- World output projected **2.2% in 2009**, ~¾ pp below Oct 2008; advanced economies to contract ¼% in 2009 — first annual contraction in the post-war period; EMs ~5% in 2009.
- Oil prices down over 50% from peak; baseline oil price for 2009 cut from $100 to $68.

## WEO Update January 2009 — *Global Economic Slump Challenges Policies*
URL: https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/weo/2009/update/01/index.htm (HTML)
- World growth projected **½% in 2009** (PPP weights), lowest since World War II; negative at market exchange rates; revision of ~1¾ pp from Nov 2008; recovery to 3% in 2010.
- Calls for credible loss recognition, triage of financial firms, capital injections, carving out bad assets.
- Global output and trade "plummeted" in the final months of 2008.

## WEO April 2009 — *Crisis and Recovery*
URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/2009/01/pdf/_exesumpdf.pdf
- p. xv: global economy in a severe recession; world output projected to **decline 1.3% in 2009**, recover to 1.9% in 2010.
- p. xv: advanced economies' real GDP fell at an unprecedented 7½% rate in 2008 Q4 (annualised); emerging economies contracted 4% in Q4 in aggregate.
- p. xv: cites April 2009 GFSR writedowns ($2.7 trn US-originated; ~$4 trn global, two-thirds banks).

## WEO October 2009 — *Sustaining the Recovery*
URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/weo/2009/02/pdf/_exesumpdf.pdf and _c1pdf.pdf
- p. xiv: steepest drop in global activity and trade since World War II; strong policies "all but eliminated fears of a global depression"; after contracting about 1% in 2009, global activity forecast to expand ~3% in 2010.
- Ch. 1 p. 2, Table 1.1: world 2008 3.0; **2009 −1.1**, 2010 3.1; advanced economies 2009 −3.4; US 2009 −2.7; world trade volume 2009 −11.9.

## GFSR April 2007 — *Market Developments and Issues*
URL: https://www.imf.org/-/media/websites/imf/imported-flagship-issues/external/pubs/ft/gfsr/2007/01/pdf/_summarypdf.pdf
- p. ix: subprime segment showing credit-quality deterioration; fallout limited so far, but scope to deepen and spread to structured mortgage products held by global investors; housing slowdown impact limited so far, "suggesting that the financial effects may also be contained" (paraphrase).
- p. x: none of the individually identified risks by themselves threaten financial stability; but a "volatility shock" could be amplified by linkages; Ch. III notes globalisation of banks may reduce individual bank risk without necessarily raising system resilience.
