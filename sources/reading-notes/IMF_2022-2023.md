# Reading notes: IMF WEO, WEO Updates and GFSR, April 2022 – October 2023 (read 22 Nov 2026)

Method: issue pages fetched from imf.org with plain `curl` (default user-agent, no custom headers); chapter files taken from the links on each issue page,
except where noted. The `.pdf.md` twins were not used (machine summaries without page numbers); every quote and page number below comes from the PDF,
converted with `pdftotext` (plain and `-layout`). Page numbers are the printed numbers from the running heads/footers
("International Monetary Fund | Month Year  N"); "p. ?" where the document prints none (then the PDF page is given).
Quotes are verbatim and under 15 words, checked by script against the converted text of the cited page; everything else is paraphrase.
Growth figures are annual percent change at PPP weights unless stated. India figures are on a fiscal-year basis (table footnote in every edition;
e.g. "2022" = FY2022/23).

## WEO April 2022 — *War Sets Back the Global Recovery* (issue page title: "World Economic Outlook, April 2022: War Sets Back The Global Recovery")
Read: Foreword (pp. xiii–xv), Executive Summary (pp. xvi–xviii), Chapter 1 "Global Prospects and Policies" (pp. 1–24 main text; Table 1.1; risk and policy sections), from text.pdf.
Note: the first page of the Executive Summary carries a stray running head from an older edition ("TENSIONS FROM THE TWO-SPEED RECOVERY"). Do not cite it.
- Foreword p. xiii (Pierre-Olivier Gourinchas, Economic Counsellor and Director of Research): outlook deteriorated since January largely because of Russia's invasion of Ukraine and the sanctions; "the war will severely set back the global recovery". Global growth projected at 3.6% in 2022 and 2023, 0.8 and 0.2 points below January.
- Foreword p. xiii: the war's effects spread "like seismic waves that emanate from the epicenter of an earthquake", mainly through commodity markets, trade and financial links; Russia a major supplier of oil, gas and metals and, with Ukraine, of wheat and corn. More than 4 million Ukrainians displaced to neighbouring countries.
- Foreword p. xiii: lockdowns in China, including in key manufacturing hubs, could cause new supply-chain bottlenecks. The Fed and Latin American central banks were already under pressure before the war and brought tightening forward.
- Foreword p. xiv: in the United States and some European countries inflation "has reached its highest level in more than 40 years", with tight labour markets; rising risk of de-anchored expectations and a more aggressive central-bank response.
- Foreword p. xiv: capital outflows from EMDEs rose markedly immediately after the invasion; repricing mostly orderly so far.
- Foreword p. xiv: the war raises the risk of "a more permanent fragmentation of the world economy into geopolitical blocks" with distinct technology standards, payment systems and reserve currencies.
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: growth to slow from an estimated 6.1% in 2021 to 3.6% in 2022 and 2023; about 3.3% over the medium term. Baseline assumes the conflict stays confined to Ukraine and no further sanctions on Russia's energy sector (embargoes announced through 31 March 2022 included).
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: downside risks include a sharper slowdown in China as "a strict zero-COVID strategy is tested by Omicron".
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: 2022 inflation projected at 5.7% in AEs and 8.7% in EMDEs, 1.8 and 2.8 points above January.
- Exec. Summary pp. xvi–xvii: the fiscal-policy bullet is headed by rising interest rates and a cost-of-living squeeze (paraphrase); debt at all-time highs; support should be targeted at refugees, those hit by commodity prices and the pandemic.
- Ch. 1 p. 1: even before the invasion, broad price pressures had led central banks to tighten; China's tight real-estate policies and zero-COVID lockdowns could slow it more than projected. Medium-term EMDE output close to 6% below pre-pandemic projections.
- Ch. 1 p. 2: baseline assumes sanctions do not tighten beyond those announced by 31 March. China's central bank cut policy rates in January 2022, an exception to tightening.
- Ch. 1 p. 3: EM capital outflows in early March were "as large and fast as those seen early in the pandemic", though concentrated in a few economies; about a quarter reversed since mid-March.
- Ch. 1 p. 4: "the Ukrainian economy is expected to contract by 35 percent" in 2022. Russia: GDP down about 8.5% in 2022 and 2.3% in 2023; ruble fell close to 60% before recovering; sovereign spreads widened more than 2,500 bp; sanctions include SWIFT exclusion for some banks and the freezing of central-bank assets.
- Ch. 1 p. 4: euro area 2022 growth revised down to 2.8% (1.1 points lower), with the biggest cuts for Germany and Italy.
- Ch. 1 p. 5: India's 2022 forecast cut by 0.8 point (Japan 0.9), partly as higher oil prices weigh on consumption and investment. US cut a further 0.3 point on faster monetary tightening; Brazil had raised rates 975 bp over the past year.
- Ch. 1 Table 1.1 p. 6: world 6.1 (2021), 3.6 (2022), 3.6 (2023); AEs 5.2/3.3/2.4; US 5.7/3.7/2.3; euro area 5.3/2.8/2.3; China 8.1/4.4/5.1; **India 8.9/8.2/6.9**; Russia 4.7/–8.5/–2.3. Consumer prices AEs 3.1/5.7/2.5; EMDEs 5.9/8.7/6.5.
- Ch. 1 p. 7 (Table 1.1 footnote): oil assumed at $106.83 a barrel in 2022 and $92.63 in 2023, against $69.07 in 2021.
- Ch. 1 p. 8: wheat prices likely to rise further; "Russia and Ukraine account for close to 30 percent of global wheat exports". Commodity spike echoes the 1970s and raises the spectre of stagflation, but the oil shock is smaller and the economy less oil-intensive (pp. 8–9).
- Ch. 1 p. 11: "over 4.5 million refugees have left Ukraine since February 24", half to Poland (UNHCR). Inflation to stay elevated for longer; oil and gas prices hit hardest in 2022, food prices well into 2023.
- Ch. 1 p. 12: futures imply oil and gas prices rising 55% and 147% in 2022, then declining in 2023.
- Ch. 1 p. 19: fragmentation risk: segmentation of reserve assets and alternative cross-border payment systems could arise from fragmented production blocks.
- Ch. 1 p. 20: with "inflation at multidecade highs in the United States, the United Kingdom", the question is how high policy rates must rise; in the 1980–82 US disinflation the federal funds rate stayed above inflation long after pressures eased.

## WEO Update July 2022 — *Gloomy and More Uncertain* (issue page title: "World Economic Outlook Update, July 2022: Gloomy and More Uncertain")
Read: whole Update (running heads present, pp. 1–13). Note: the running head on p. 2 reads "STRICTLY CONFIDENTIAL"; ignore it.
- p. 1: growth to slow from 6.1% (2021) to 3.2% in 2022, 0.4 point below April; 2.9% in 2023. US revised down 1.4 points, China 1.1 points (lockdowns, deepening real-estate crisis).
- p. 1: inflation expected at 6.6% in AEs and 9.5% in EMDEs in 2022, up 0.9 and 0.8 point. Risks include a sudden stop of European gas imports from Russia; an alternative scenario has growth of about 2.6% and 2.0% in 2022 and 2023.
- p. 1: "taming inflation should be the first priority for policymakers".
- p. 2: world real GDP estimated to have shrunk in Q2 2022, the first contraction since 2020; US CPI up 9.1% year on year in June, UK 9.1% in May.
- p. 6: China growth cut to 3.3%, the lowest in more than four decades excluding 2020; India revised down 0.8 point to 7.4%, mainly on less favourable external conditions and faster policy tightening.
- Table 1 p. 7: world 3.2 (2022) / 2.9 (2023); US 2.3/1.0; euro area 2.6/1.2; China 3.3/4.6; **India 7.4/6.1**; Russia –6.0/–3.5. World consumer prices 8.3 (2022) / 5.7 (2023).

## WEO October 2022 — *Countering the Cost-of-Living Crisis* (issue page title: "World Economic Outlook, October 2022: Countering the Cost-of-Living Crisis")
Read: Foreword (pp. xiii–xv), Executive Summary (p. xvi), Chapter 1 "Global Prospects and Policies" (pp. 1–25; Table 1.1; Box 1.1), from text.pdf; Chapter 2 opening (p. 51).
Note: the Executive Summary page also carries the stray older running head ("TENSIONS FROM THE TWO-SPEED RECOVERY").
- Foreword p. xiii (Gourinchas): three forces: the Russian invasion of Ukraine, a cost-of-living crisis from persistent and broadening inflation, and China's slowdown. Growth unchanged at 3.2% in 2022, 2.7% in 2023 (0.2 below July), with a 25% chance of falling below 2%.
- Foreword p. xiii: more than a third of the global economy will contract this year or next; "the worst is yet to come".
- Foreword p. xiii: "for many people 2023 will feel like a recession".
- Foreword p. xiii: severe energy crisis in Europe: "Gas prices in Europe have increased more than four-fold since 2021", with Russia cutting deliveries to less than 20% of 2021 levels.
- Foreword p. xiii: inflation triggered rapid, synchronised tightening, alongside "a powerful appreciation of the US dollar against most other currencies". Global inflation expected to peak in late 2022 and fall to 4.1% by 2024.
- Foreword p. xiii: China's zero-COVID lockdowns hurt especially in Q2 2022; "the property sector, representing about one-fifth of economic activity in China", is rapidly weakening.
- Foreword pp. xiii–xiv: risks of both under- and over-tightening, but the costs are not symmetric: misjudging inflation's persistence again could gravely undermine central-bank credibility.
- Foreword p. xiv: the energy crisis is not transitory: "Winter 2022 will be challenging for Europe, but winter 2023 will likely be worse."
- Foreword p. xiv: "The dollar is now at its highest level since the early 2000s." Mostly driven by fundamentals (US tightening, energy crisis); let exchange rates adjust and conserve reserves.
- Foreword p. xiv: "Now is the time for emerging market policymakers to batten down the hatches." Too many low-income countries in or near debt distress.
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: inflation "higher than seen in several decades"; growth to slow from 6.0% (2021) to 3.2% (2022) and 2.7% (2023).
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: "This is the weakest growth profile since 2001 except for the global financial crisis" and the acute COVID phase; US GDP contracted in H1 2022, euro area to contract in H2 2022.
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: "About a third of the world economy faces two consecutive quarters of negative growth."
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: global inflation to rise from 4.7% (2021) to 8.8% (2022), then fall to 6.5% (2023) and 4.1% (2024). Risks include a halt to Russian gas supplies to Europe, a worsening Chinese property crisis and geopolitical fragmentation.
- Ch. 1 p. 1: "Inflation has soared to multidecade highs", squeezing household budgets; global real GDP contracted slightly in Q2 2022, with negative growth in China, Russia and the US.
- Ch. 1 p. 2: "In 2022, inflation in advanced economies reached its highest rate since 1982." Food prices were a prime driver of global inflation so far this year; Black Sea grain deal gives some hope.
- Ch. 1 p. 4: EMDE inflation estimated at 10.1% in Q2 2022, peaking at 11.0% in Q3, the highest since 1999; UK annual inflation 9.9%.
- Ch. 1 p. 5: "High inflation in 2021 and 2022 has surprised many macroeconomic forecasters, including IMF staff." Errors linked to the size of fiscal stimulus in AEs and to supply shocks; "incipient wage-price spirals are not yet on the horizon" (also p. 5).
- Ch. 1 p. 5: "The Federal Reserve has increased the federal funds target rate by 3 percentage points" since early 2022; Bank of England up 2 points this year, ECB 1.25 points. Real policy rates mostly still below pre-pandemic levels.
- Ch. 1 p. 6: sanctions and reduced Russian exports, with "the flow of Russian pipeline gas to Europe down to about 20 percent" of its year-earlier level.
- Ch. 1 p. 7: Russia's economy estimated to have contracted 21.8% (annualised) in Q2. Food about 40% of the sub-Saharan Africa consumption basket.
- Ch. 1 p. 8: a global recession is not in the baseline, but a "technical recession" (two quarters of contraction) is expected in some economies.
- Ch. 1 Table 1.1 p. 9: world 6.0 (2021), 3.2 (2022), 2.7 (2023); AEs 5.2/2.4/1.1; US 5.7/1.6/1.0; euro area 5.2/3.1/0.5; Germany 2.6/1.5/–0.3; China 8.1/3.2/4.4; **India 8.7/6.8/6.1**; Russia 4.7/–3.4/–2.3. World consumer prices 4.7/8.8/6.5 (AEs 3.1/7.2/4.4; EMDEs 5.9/9.9/8.1).
- Ch. 1 p. 12: China's 2022 growth of 3.2% is "the lowest growth in more than four decades", excluding 2020. India 6.8% in 2022 (0.6 point down from July, weak Q2 and softer external demand) and 6.1% in 2023 (unchanged).
- Ch. 1 p. 14: on a four-quarter basis, global headline inflation peaks at 9.5% in Q3 2022, declining to 4.7% by Q4 2023.
- Ch. 1 p. 15: the dollar up about 13% in nominal effective terms (September versus 2021 average), likely slowing world trade.
- Ch. 1 p. 18: in 2022 the dollar appreciated about 15% against the euro, over 10% against the renminbi, 25% against the yen and 20% against sterling; Japan intervened in September to support the yen.
- Ch. 1 p. 20: probability of one-year-ahead global growth below 2.0% about 25%, more than double normal; below 2% has happened only five times since 1970 (1973, 1981, 1982, 2009, 2020).
- Ch. 1 Box 1.1 p. 26 ("Dissecting Recent WEO Inflation Forecast Errors"): inflation repeatedly exceeded WEO forecasts in 2021–22; errors averaged 3.2 points globally (1.7 for Europe), which "compare with a near-zero average for the decade that preceded the COVID-19 crisis".
- Ch. 2 p. 51 ("Wage Dynamics Post–COVID-19 and Wage-Price Spiral Risks", summary): historical episodes with flat or falling real wages did not tend to show a subsequent spiral; front-loaded tightening lowers de-anchoring risk; "the chances of persistent wage-price spirals emerging appear limited".

## WEO April 2023 — *A Rocky Recovery* (issue page title: "World Economic Outlook, April 2023: A Rocky Recovery")
Read: Foreword (pp. xiv–xv), Executive Summary (pp. xvi–xvii), Chapter 1 "Global Prospects and Policies" (ch1.pdf, pp. 1–22, Table 1.1); Chapter 2 opening (p. 45).
- Foreword p. xiv (Gourinchas), titled "It Was Never Going to Be an Easy Ride": growth bottoms at 2.8% this year, then 3.0% in 2024; global inflation 8.7% (2022), 7.0% (2023), 4.9% (2024).
- Foreword p. xiv: "Inflation is much stickier than anticipated even a few months ago." Core inflation not yet peaked in many countries.
- Foreword p. xiv: on a wage-price spiral: "At this point, I remain unconvinced." Real wages have fallen steeply; margins can absorb rising labour costs.
- Foreword p. xiv: tightening is having side effects: the UK gilt-market instability last autumn and US banking turbulence with "the collapse of a few regional banks".
- Foreword pp. xiv–xv: investors look for the next weakest link, as with Credit Suisse, a globally systemic but ailing European bank. In a severe downside scenario global GDP per capita could nearly fall, with probability about 15%.
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: tentative early-2023 hopes of a soft landing receded amid sticky inflation and financial-sector turmoil. Baseline (stresses contained): growth from 3.4% (2022) to 2.8% (2023), settling at 3.0% five years out, "the lowest medium-term forecast in decades". AEs from 2.7% to 1.3%; alternative scenario about 2.5% growth.
- Exec. Summary p. xvi: headline inflation from 8.7% to 7.0%; return to target unlikely before 2025 in most cases; "the chances of a hard landing having risen sharply".
- Ch. 1 p. 1: "inflation reached multidecade highs last year in many economies". The "failures of two specialized regional banks in the United States in mid-March 2023" (unnamed in the WEO text) and the collapse of confidence in Credit Suisse roiled markets.
- Ch. 1 p. 1: "The loss of confidence in Credit Suisse resulted in a brokered takeover." Bank equities under extreme pressure.
- Ch. 1 p. 3: headline inflation nearly 7% in the euro area (some members near 15%) and above 10% in the UK; "no signs of a wage-price spiral dynamic" taking hold.
- Ch. 1 p. 5: invasion of Ukraine dated to February 2022; Europe more resilient than expected; EU support about 1.3% of GDP; gas flows reoriented to non-Russian pipelines and LNG.
- Ch. 1 p. 6: China lifted COVID restrictions; large outbreaks cut activity in Q4 2022; property stresses and a backlog of presold unfinished housing; reopening likely to produce positive spillovers (China takes about a quarter of Asia's exports).
- Ch. 1 Table 1.1 p. 9: world 3.4 (2022), 2.8 (2023), 3.0 (2024); AEs 2.7/1.3/1.4; US 2.1/1.6/1.1; euro area 3.5/0.8/1.4; UK 4.0/–0.3/1.0; China 3.0/5.2/4.5; **India 6.8/5.9/6.3**; Russia –2.1/0.7/1.3. World consumer prices 8.7/7.0/4.9 (AEs 7.3/4.7/2.6; EMDEs 9.8/8.6/6.5). Chapter 1 text gives no separate remark on India.
- Ch. 1 p. 10 (footnote): oil averaged $96.36 a barrel in 2022; assumed $73.13 in 2023.
- Ch. 1 p. 12: medium-term growth of 3.0% is "the lowest medium-term growth forecast published in all WEO reports since 1990".
- Ch. 1 p. 16: "For the winter of 2022–23, a gas crisis was averted", thanks to LNG imports, lower demand and mild weather; the war has split the world economy into geopolitical blocs.
- Ch. 1 p. 18: warns against premature easing, citing the US early 1980s when the Fed loosened after a first wave of tightening.
- Ch. 1 p. 19: enhanced dollar swap lines between the Fed and major AE central banks should limit financial strains.
- Ch. 2 p. 45 ("The Natural Rate of Interest: Drivers and Implications for Policy", summary): demographics and the productivity slowdown drove the decline in the natural rate; once inflation passes, "interest rates are likely to revert toward pre-pandemic levels in advanced economies".

## GFSR April 2023 — *Safeguarding Financial Stability amid High Inflation and Geopolitical Risks* (issue page title: "Global Financial Stability Report, April 2023: Safeguarding Financial Stability amid High Inflation and Geopolitical Risks")
Read: Executive Summary "A Financial System Tested by Higher Inflation and Interest Rates" (pp. xii–xvii); Chapter 1, same title (pp. 1–7). Data cut-off 30 March 2023 (Ch. 1 p. 2, footnote 1).
- Exec. Summary p. xii: "Financial stability risks have risen significantly" since October 2022; the sudden failures of Silicon Valley Bank and Signature Bank and the loss of confidence in Credit Suisse, a GSIB.
- Exec. Summary p. xii: social media helped isolated US events spread worldwide; rate-expectation repricing comparable in magnitude to Black Monday in 1987.
- Exec. Summary p. xii: US regulators guaranteed uninsured deposits at the two failed banks and created a Bank Term Funding Program; the Swiss National Bank gave Credit Suisse emergency liquidity before its state-supported takeover by UBS.
- Exec. Summary p. xii: the question is whether these events are "a canary in the coal mine" or isolated.
- Exec. Summary p. xiii: lending capacity of US banks could decline by almost 1% in the coming year, cutting real GDP by 44 basis points.
- Ch. 1 p. 4: SVB's failure depegged two stablecoins (USDC and Dai) and contributed to Signature Bank's demise over its crypto footprint.
- Ch. 1 p. 5: SVB disclosed a $1.8 billion securities loss on 6 March and a $2.25 billion stock offering on 8 March; "$42 billion of deposit withdrawals followed on March 9"; FDIC took control on 10 March. Signature lost 20% of deposits and was closed on 12 March.
- Ch. 1 p. 5: despite the ECB's 50 bp hike on 16 March and the Fed's 25 bp rise on 22 March, investors sharply repriced the expected policy path downward.
- Ch. 1 p. 7: Credit Suisse lost investor confidence in mid-March; on 19 March it was "taken over by rival UBS at a price tag of 3 billion Swiss francs", with a CHF 9 billion state guarantee; all CHF 16 billion of AT1 debt written down while shareholders recovered CHF 3 billion.

## WEO October 2023 — *Navigating Global Divergences* (issue page title: "World Economic Outlook, October 2023: Navigating Global Divergences")
Read: Foreword (pp. xiii–xv), Executive Summary (p. xvi), Chapter 1 "Global Prospects and Policies" (ch1.pdf, pp. 1–24, Table 1.1).
- Foreword p. xiii (Gourinchas), titled "Resilient Global Economy Is Limping Along, with Growing Divergences": despite the war and unprecedented tightening against decades-high inflation, the economy slowed but did not stall: "The global economy is limping along, not sprinting."
- Foreword p. xiii: growth from 3.5% (2022) to 3% (2023) and 2.9% (2024); headline inflation from 9.2% in 2022 (year over year basis) to 5.9% and 4.8%; projections increasingly consistent with a soft landing, especially in the US.
- Foreword p. xiv: China's real-estate crisis is a key risk; oil up about 25% since June on OPEC+ cuts.
- Foreword p. xiv: fiscal and monetary policy less aligned in 2023: "Most worrying is the case of the United States", where the fiscal stance deteriorated substantially.

- Ch. 1 p. 4: China's momentum fading after the reopening surge (8.9% annualised in Q1 2023); Country Garden, the largest property developer, facing severe liquidity stress.
- Ch. 1 p. 4: "Global headline inflation has more than halved", from a peak of 11.6% in Q2 2022 (quarterly annualised) to 5.3% in Q2 2023.
- Ch. 1 p. 8: "The March 2023 banking scare remained contained" to US regional banks and Credit Suisse. The Bank of Canada, Bank of England, ECB and Fed all raised rates in July; the Bank of Japan loosened yield-curve control.
- Ch. 1 Table 1.1 p. 12: world 3.5 (2022), 3.0 (2023), 2.9 (2024); AEs 2.6/1.5/1.4; US 2.1/2.1/1.5; euro area 3.3/0.7/1.2; Germany 1.8/–0.5/0.9; China 3.0/5.0/4.2; **India 7.2/6.3/6.3**; Russia –2.1/2.2/1.1. World consumer prices 8.7/6.9/5.8 (AEs 7.3/4.6/3.0; EMDEs 9.8/8.5/7.8).
- Ch. 1 p. 14: US unemployment to peak at 4.0% by Q4 2024, lower than projected in April 2023 (5.2%) and October 2022 (5.6%): a softer landing than expected.
- Ch. 1 p. 15: India projected at 6.3% in both 2023 and 2024, up 0.2 point for 2023 on stronger consumption in April–June. China's 2023 forecast cut, mainly on lower investment amid the property crisis.
- Ch. 1 p. 16: global headline inflation peaked at 9.5% (year over year) in Q3 2022; US to fall from 7.1% in Q4 2022 to 3.2% in Q4 2023.
- Ch. 1 p. 18: almost 3,000 new trade restrictions imposed in 2022, against fewer than 1,000 in 2019.

## Hindsight: inflation and growth forecasts against later figures (derived from the tables above; no new source)
- 2022 inflation: April 2022 WEO projected 5.7% (AEs) and 8.7% (EMDEs) (Exec. Summary p. xvi). The April 2023 WEO (Table 1.1 p. 9) and October 2023 WEO (Table 1.1 p. 12) give 7.3% and 9.8%, with world inflation of 8.7%. So the April 2022 forecast was about 1.6 points too low for AEs and 1.1 points too low for EMDEs. The October 2022 forecast for world 2022 inflation (8.8%, Table 1.1 p. 9) was close to the later 8.7%.
