# India: Economic Surveys 2024-25 and 2025-26 — reading notes

These notes cover the India section of Phase 8, "AI, tariffs and fragmentation" (calendar 2024–2025). In fiscal-year terms that is FY2024-25 (FY25) and FY2025-26 (FY26). Two Surveys are read here: ES 2024-25 (January 2025) and ES 2025-26 (January 2026). **Numbering restarts in this file.** The earlier file, `India_ES_2022-2024.md`, already used ES24-1 to ES24-4 for four retrospective items from ES 2024-25. The ES24-n items below are new, and ES24-1 here is not the same item as ES24-1 there. When citing across the two files, write "ES24-n (2024-26 notes)".

## Economic Survey 2024-25 (January 2025)

### The setting

**ES24-1 — The Preface's framing: elections, a new US administration, China's surplus.** Preface, p. vii (st. 8).
- 2024 was a year of elections around the world, and India returned the incumbent government for a third term.
- At the time of writing, the new US President had been in office less than two weeks. There was an early sign of policy changes on the movement of goods and labour.
- India's export growth is called a high-beta play on global export growth, so domestic levers matter more.
- The Preface names the "China Challenge", with solar supply chains as the example.

Quote (Preface): "China’s trade surplus in 2024 was nearly one trillion US dollars."

**ES24-2 — Deregulation as the Survey's main message.** Preface, p. ix (st. 10). The Preface's theme is "getting out of the way", meaning the state should move from presuming guilt to presuming innocence in how it regulates business. Ch 5 (paras 5.39–5.45, pp. 149–150, st. 194–195) argues that deregulation and economic freedom matter most for MSMEs.

Quote (Preface): "‘guilty until proven innocent’ to ‘innocent until proven guilty’"

### Growth and the mid-2024 slowdown

**ES24-3 — FY25 growth estimate: 6.4%.** Ch 1 summary, p. 1 (st. 46); paras 1.22–1.23, p. 11 (st. 56).
- The NSO's First Advance Estimate puts FY25 real GDP growth at 6.4%. Real GVA also grows 6.4%.
- By sector: agriculture 3.8%, industry 6.2%, services 7.2%.
- Private consumption (PFCE) grows 7.3%. Its share of GDP is 61.8%, the highest since FY03.
- Fixed investment (GFCF) grows 6.4%.
- Manufacturing faced weak global demand.

Quote (Ch 1 summary): "India’s real GDP is estimated to grow by 6.4 per cent in FY25"

FLAG (vintage): ES 2025-26 revises FY25 to 6.5% (PE) (ES25-3).

**ES24-4 — The Q2 dip and its causes.** Ch 1, paras 1.28–1.30 and 1.35, pp. 14–17 (st. 59–62).
- Quarterly GDP: 6.7% in Q1 FY25 and 5.4% in Q2, so 6.0% for H1 (para 1.35). Real GVA grew 6.2% in H1 (para 1.28).
- Industry: 8.3% in Q1 and 6% for H1.
- The Survey gives three reasons for the Q2 slowdown:
  - manufacturing exports slowed, because of weak demand and the aggressive trade and industrial policies of major trading nations;
  - heavy monsoon rains disrupted mining and construction;
  - the timing of the festivals.

Quote (para 1.28): "followed by a subdued performance in Q2 FY25"

FLAG (vintage): ES 2025-26 Table I.2a shows H1 FY25 GDP growth as 6.1%, not 6.0%.

**ES24-5 — The election pause in public capex.** Ch 1, paras 1.38, 1.40 and 1.45, pp. 18–21 (st. 63–66).
- GFCF growth slowed from 10.1% in H1 FY24 to 6.4% in H1 FY25 (para 1.38). Part of the reason was a capex slowdown in Q1 during the general elections.
- Union capex picked up after July: it was up 8.2% in July–November 2024 (para 1.40).
- Gross tax revenue rose 10.7% in April–November 2024 (para 1.45).

Quote (para 1.45): "it remained subdued during Q1 FY25, owing to general elections"

**ES24-6 — FY26 forecast: 6.3–6.8%.** Ch 1, para 1.72, p. 33 (st. 78).

Quote (para 1.72): "growth in FY26 would be between 6.3 and 6.8 per cent"

FLAG (forecast vs outcome): ES 2025-26 puts FY26 at 7.4% (FAE), above the top of this range (ES25-3).

### Inflation and monetary policy

**ES24-7 — Headline inflation falls, food inflation rises.** Ch 1, paras 1.52–1.53, p. 25 (st. 70).
- CPI inflation fell from 5.4% in FY24 to 4.9% in April–December 2024. Core inflation fell 0.9 points.
- Food inflation (CFPI) rose from 7.5% to 8.4%.
- Box IV.1 (Ch 4) lists food measures, including a 20% export duty on onions from 13 September 2024.

Quote (para 1.53): "has increased from 7.5 per cent in FY24 to 8.4 per cent in FY25"

FLAG (definition): the "FY25" in this quote means April–December 2024, not the full year.

**ES24-8 — RBI holds the repo rate, changes its stance, cuts the CRR.** Ch 2, para 2.4, p. 36 (st. 81–82).
- The repo rate stayed at 6.5% through April–December 2024.
- In October 2024 the Monetary Policy Committee changed its stance from "withdrawal of accommodation" to "neutral".
- In December 2024 the RBI cut the cash reserve ratio (CRR) from 4.5% to 4% of NDTL, releasing about ₹1.16 lakh crore.
- Ch 4 (para 4.22, p. 129, st. 174): in December 2024 the RBI raised its FY25 inflation projection from 4.5% to 4.8% and projected 4.2% for FY26. The IMF projected 4.4% and 4.1%.

Quote (para 2.4): "decided to change the policy stance from the ‘withdrawal of accommodation’ to ‘neutral’"

FLAG (forecast vs outcome): FY25 CPI came in at 4.6% (ES25-12). The RBI's FY26 projection went from 4.2% (December 2024) to 2.0% (December 2025), and the April–December outcome was 1.7%.

### External sector

**ES24-9 — Trade policy uncertainty and the exposed sectors.** Ch 1, paras 1.21 and 1.59, pp. 10–11 and 28 (st. 55–56, 73).
- Trade policy uncertainty rose sharply but was not yet at 2018-19 levels.
- G20 import-restrictive measures covered 12.7% of G20 imports, more than three times the 2015 level.
- The trade stance of major economies could hit chemicals, machinery, textiles and electronics.
- Ch 3 adds two more points:
  - friend-shoring and nearshoring (para 3.7, p. 82);
  - the EU's CBAM levy, which applies from 1 January 2026 (Box III.2, around p. 88, st. 133).

Quote (para 1.59): "evolving trade stance of a few major economies could affect key Indian export sectors"

**ES24-10 — Trade, services and the current account in FY25 so far.** Ch 1, paras 1.54–1.55, pp. 26–27 (st. 71–72); Ch 3 summary, p. 79 (st. 124); paras 3.34–3.46, pp. 99–105 (st. 144–150).
- Merchandise exports +1.6% (non-oil +7.1%) and imports +5.2% in April–December 2024.
- Total exports USD 602.6bn (+6%) and imports USD 682.2bn (+6.9%) in the first nine months.
- Services exports +11.6%. India had 4.3% of world services exports in 2023.
- The current-account deficit (CAD) was 1.2% of GDP in Q2 FY25.
- Private transfers rose from USD 28.1bn to 31.9bn (Q2).
- India's average tariff on dutiable items fell from 48.9% in 2000 to 17.3% in 2024 (para 3.12, p. 84).

Quote (para 1.55): "remains relatively contained at 1.2 per cent of GDP in Q2 FY25"

**ES24-11 — FDI nearly vanishes in net terms; FPI slows; reserves.** Ch 1, paras 1.56–1.58, pp. 27–28 (st. 72–73); Ch 3, para 3.53, p. 108 (st. 153).
- FDI:
  - Gross FDI rose 17.9% in April–November 2024.
  - Repatriation rose 33.2%, after 51.5% in FY24.
  - Net FDI was USD 0.48bn, against USD 8.5bn a year earlier.
  - Full-year net FDI was USD 19.8bn in FY23 and USD 10.1bn in FY24.
- FPI: net inflows were USD 10.6bn in April–December 2024, against USD 31.7bn a year earlier.
- Reserves:
  - USD 616.7bn at end-January 2024, peaking at USD 704.9bn in September 2024, and USD 634.6bn on 3 January 2025;
  - enough to cover 90% of external debt and more than 10 months of imports.

FLAG (vintage): ES 2025-26 gives net FDI for April–November 2024 as USD 0.8bn (ES25-15).

FLAG (internal): the Ch 3 summary gives reserves of USD 640.3bn at end-December 2024. Para 1.58 gives USD 634.6bn on 3 January 2025. The dates differ, so this may not be an error.

**ES24-12 — The rupee in FY25.** Ch 3, para 3.66, p. 114 (st. 159). The rupee fell 2.9% against the dollar in FY25 up to 6 January 2025. The Survey compares the Canadian dollar (5.4%), the Korean won (8.2%) and the Brazilian real (17.4%), and puts the fall down to dollar strength.

Quote (para 3.66): "the INR depreciated a modest 2.9 per cent"

FLAG (forecast vs outcome): ES 2025-26 reports a fall of 5.4–6.5% in FY26 (ES25-16).

### Medium term, China and Viksit Bharat

**ES24-13 — Viksit Bharat needs about 8% growth.** Ch 5, paras 5.1–5.4 and 5.42, pp. 133–135 and 150 (st. 178–180, 195).
- Developed-country status by the centenary of Independence needs about 8% growth for a decade or two.
- The investment rate would have to rise from about 31% to about 35% of GDP.
- Citing the IMF's October 2024 WEO, India reaches USD 5 trillion by FY28 and USD 6.307 trillion by FY30.
- These projections assume:
  - growth of about 6.5% over FY26–FY30;
  - rupee depreciation of 0.5% a year, against 3.3% historically;
  - the CAD rising to 2.2% of GDP by FY30.

Quote (para 5.1): "India needs to achieve a growth rate of around 8 per cent"

**ES24-14 — China's manufacturing weight; outsourcing "reset".** Ch 5, paras 5.18–5.19, pp. 140–141 (st. 185–186). UNIDO projects China at 45% of global manufacturing by 2030. The Survey argues that the globalisation-era model of outsourcing manufacturing to China is being reset. It does not use the phrase "China plus one" (see Not found).

Quote (para 5.19): "outsourcing manufacturing to China pursued vigorously in the globalisation era is poised for a reset"

### AI, jobs and data centres

**ES24-15 — Ch 13: AI and labour.** Ch 13 summary, p. 411 (st. 456); paras 13.11, 13.14 and 13.41, pp. 415–424 (st. 460–469).
- IIM Ahmedabad survey: 68% of white-collar workers expect their jobs to be partly or fully automated within five years.
- NASSCOM projects the AI market growing at a 25–35% CAGR to 2027.
- India needs about 78.5 lakh non-farm jobs a year to 2030.
- Much of the IT workforce is in low-value-added services, which are the most exposed.
- Data centres already use more electricity than Italy, and could use up to 1580 TWh, as much as India uses.
- The Preface (p. xi) suggests, tentatively, that some fears may be overdone.

Quote (Ch 13 summary): "AI can serve not as a crisis but as a catalyst"

**ES24-16 — Not in this Survey.** ES 2024-25 has no fiscal chapter. Its text gives no Union fiscal deficit figures for FY25, and the SA Table 2.4 has no FY25 column (see Not found). It came out before the Budget 2025-26 income-tax changes and before the 2025 US tariffs.

## Economic Survey 2025-26 (January 2026)

### The setting

**ES25-1 — Preface: tariffs, rating upgrades, a year of reform.** Preface, p. vii (st. 8).
- Fiscal:
  - The FY25 fiscal deficit was 4.8% of GDP, against 4.9% budgeted. The FY26 target is 4.4%.
  - That is less than half the 9.2% of FY21.
- Rating upgrades:
  - Morningstar DBRS (May);
  - S&P, from BBB- to BBB (August), the first upgrade from a major agency in nearly two decades;
  - R&I (September).
- US tariffs:
  - "Reciprocal" tariffs of 25% on India were announced in April.
  - In August came an additional penal tariff of 25% on most merchandise exports.
- Growth forecasts for India were cut, but growth sped up.
- Reforms: GST overhaul; nuclear power opened to private firms; 100% FDI allowed in insurance; four labour codes notified; Quality Control Orders put on hold.
- The rupee did badly in 2025.

Quote (Preface, on the tariffs): "it surprised many since India was expected to be one of the early winners"

FLAG (internal): the Preface says the reciprocal 25% was "announced in April". Ch 4 footnote 26 cites a US Executive Order of 31 July 2025 for the reciprocal tariff and one of 6 August 2025 for the additional tariff. These may be announcement and legal instrument, but the text does not say so.

**ES25-2 — The rupee "punching below its weight"; three scenarios for 2026.** Preface, pp. viii–x (st. 9–11).
- The Preface says the rupee underperformed the economy.
- Gold rose from USD 2607 to USD 4315 an ounce in 2025.
- It sets out three scenarios for 2026, with probabilities of 40–45%, 40–45% and 10–20%. One of the risks it names is leverage in AI data centres (citing the FT: about $120bn off balance sheet).
- The worst case is a systemic shock that could be worse than 2008.

Quote (Preface, p. x): "The macroeconomic consequences could be worse than those of the 2008 global financial crisis."

Second quote, kept for the record only: Preface, p. viii, "the rupee, therefore, is punching below its weight". If both quotes are needed, use them in separate sections of the book.

### Growth and its vintages

**ES25-3 — FY26 growth 7.4%; FY25 revised to 6.5%.** Ch 1 summary, p. 1 (st. 52); para 1.8 and Table I.2a, pp. 8–9 (st. 59–60); paras 1.9 and 1.12, pp. 10–11 (st. 61–63).
- The FY26 FAE is 7.4%, against 6.5% (PE) for FY25.
- H1 FY26 grew 8.0%, against 6.1% in H1 FY25.
- GVA 7.3%; manufacturing 7.0% (4.5% in FY25); services 9.1% (7.2% in FY25).
- India is the fastest-growing major economy for the fourth year running.
- Private consumption is 61.5% of GDP, the highest since FY12. GFCF is 30.0% of GDP.
- Private investment announcements were ₹14.6 lakh crore in H1 FY26.
- A nowcast puts Q3 FY26 at about 7% (Box I.2, p. 21, st. 72).

Quote (Ch 1 summary): "The First Advance Estimates place real GDP growth at 7.4 per cent"

FLAG (forecast vs outcome): ES 2024-25 projected 6.3–6.8% for FY26. ES 2025-26 says it beat those projections.

FLAG (vintage): FY25 went from 6.4% (FAE, ES24) to 6.5% (PE). In the SA levels, 2022-23 goes from 7.0% to 7.6% and 2023-24 from 8.2% to 9.2% (derived; see the cross-Survey table).

**ES25-4 — The rebasing is coming.** Ch 1, Box I.3, pp. 22–23 (st. 73–74). New base years, with release dates:
- GDP: base 2022-23, 27 February 2026;
- CPI: base 2024, 12 February 2026;
- IIP: 28 May 2026.

Quote (Box I.3): "rebasing of National accounts to 2022-23, scheduled for release on 27 February 2026"

FLAG (vintage): every FY25 and FY26 national-accounts and CPI figure in these notes predates the rebasing. Check the book's growth figures against the new series.

**ES25-5 — FY27 outlook and potential growth of 7%.** Ch 1, paras 1.42–1.46 and Box I.4, pp. 30–35 (st. 81–86); Preface, p. xiv (st. 15).
- FY27 is projected at 6.8–7.2%.
- Para 1.42 calls FY26 "unusually challenging" because of the "high, penal tariffs", and FY27 a year of adjustment.
- Potential growth is revised up from 6.5% (ES 2022-23) to about 7%.
- The decomposition for FY26–FY30 (Table I.7) is capital 7.6, labour 2.6 and TFP 1.9, giving 7.0. The text does not explain how the units add up.
- Para 1.43 names the risk of a correction in the AI boom.

Quote (para 1.46): "real GDP growth in FY27 in the range of 6.8 to 7.2 per cent"

FLAG (forecast vs outcome): potential growth goes from 6.5% to 7.0%. This Survey does not restate ES 2024-25's "about 8% for Viksit Bharat" (see Not found).

### US tariffs, trade deals and fragmentation

**ES25-6 — US tariffs as the year's biggest shock.** Ch 1, para 1.1 and Table I.1, p. 2 (st. 53).
- The reciprocal tariffs of April 2025 are put at the centre of the global story.
- The IMF's forecast for 2025 world growth moved with them:
  - 3.3% (January 2025 WEO);
  - 2.8% (April 2025);
  - 3.2% (October 2025);
  - 3.3% (January 2026).
- Box I.1 (pp. 4–7, st. 56–59) covers economic statecraft:
  - US chip export controls;
  - China's rare-earth curbs;
  - the EU's CBAM, which takes full effect in 2026.

Quote (para 1.1): "The most disruptive amongst these disturbances was the imposition of tariffs by the USA"

**ES25-7 — An effective 50% tariff, and its effect by sector.** Ch 4, para 4.40 and Table IV.1, pp. 159–162 (st. 210–213).
- Goods exported to the US face an effective tariff rate of 50%, among the highest.
- Footnote 27: there have been six rounds of negotiation. The US exempted more than 200 farm and food products (PIB, 26 September 2025).
- Table IV.1 (April–November, year on year):

| Sector | To the US | To the world |
|---|---|---|
| Gems and jewellery | −44.3% (US share 33.7% → 18.7%) | +0.6% |
| Marine products | −5.7% | +16.1% |
| Auto components | −6.8% | +6.0% |
| Textiles | −6.1% | +0.3% |
| Pharma | +0.8% (−23.7% in October, +9.8% in November) | +6.5% |
| Leather | −2.6% | +0.6% |
| Paper | −2.1% | −1.2% |

Quote (para 4.40): "an effective export tariff rate of 50 per cent on goods exported to the US"

FLAG (definition): the main text never says why the extra 25% was imposed. Footnote 26 only cites the title of the 6 August 2025 Executive Order, which refers to the Russian Federation. Do not supply a reason from memory.

**ES25-8 — Crude imports shift towards the US.** Ch 4, para 4.42, p. 163 (st. 214). The US share of India's crude imports rose from 4.6% to 8.1% (April–November). Imports from Russia, Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Venezuela fell. The text gives no figures for Russian crude.

Quote (para 4.42): "while those from Russia, Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Venezuela declined"

**ES25-9 — Using the crisis for reform.** Ch 1, para 1.42, p. 30 (st. 81); Ch 16, paras 16.2–16.5, p. 629 (st. 680); Ch 4, paras 4.53–4.54, p. 169 (st. 220).
- The tariff shock was used to push through GST rationalisation and deregulation.
- After the "reciprocal tariffs and penal tariffs" of April and August, the government:
  - stepped up negotiations;
  - announced a relief package and a five-year Export Promotion Mission;
  - had the RBI give exporters relief: a moratorium, export credit for up to 450 days, and a longer time to realise export proceeds (9 → 15 months).

Quote (para 1.42): "using this crisis as an opportunity to push through key measures"

**ES25-10 — Trade agreements.** Preface, p. xii (st. 13); Ch 1, paras 1.38 and 1.44, p. 28 (st. 79) and following; Ch 4, para 4.51, p. 168 (st. 219).
- UK CETA: "recently concluded".
- Oman CEPA: signed 18 December 2025.
- New Zealand: concluded December 2025.
- Still being negotiated: the US, Chile and Peru. Para 1.44 expects the US talks to conclude during the year.
- EFTA TEPA: carries a USD 100bn investment commitment (Box IV.3).
- The EU: the Preface says the FTA was concluded after three years of talks and still needs ratification by the European Parliament.

Quote (Preface): "free trade agreement with the European Union after three years of negotiations"

FLAG (internal): the EU FTA is "concluded" in the Preface and refers to "signing" in para 1.38. It is not in the para 4.51 list, which was probably written earlier.

**ES25-11 — Fragmentation: "geostrategic globalisation" and China's scale.** Ch 4, paras 4.2, 4.7–4.11, pp. 145–150 (st. 196–201); Preface, p. xii (st. 13).
- Trade policy uncertainty and global economic policy uncertainty both peaked in April 2025.
- Trade policy uncertainty was up 1165.6% year on year, the highest since 1960; global economic policy uncertainty was up 228.2%, the highest since 1997 (para 4.11).
- Friend-shoring came back in 2025 (para 4.8).
- Para 4.7 describes a move away from unrestricted integration: supply chains are now judged on resilience and strategic autonomy, not just cost.
- The Preface argues:
  - Global manufacturing is anchored in China's scale.
  - Lowering the cost of capital for upstream inputs is better than raising import protection.
- IMF world trade volume: 3.5% (2024), 3.6% (2025), 2.3% (2026) (para 4.15).

Quote (para 4.7): "from unfettered integration to ‘geostrategic globalisation’"

A second phrase, kept for the record only (Preface, p. xii): "global manufacturing is anchored in China’s scale and integrated industrial systems".

### Inflation and monetary policy

**ES25-12 — Inflation falls to a series low.** Ch 1, para 1.29, p. 24 (st. 75); Ch 5, paras 5.8–5.14 and 5.36, pp. 207–222 (st. 258–273).
- CPI inflation by year: 6.7% (FY23), 4.6% (FY25), 1.7% (April–December FY26), the lowest in the series.
- The fall began in October 2024 (6.2%).
- Monthly path: 3.2% in April 2025, 1.4% in September, 0.3% in October.
- Core inflation was 4.62% in December 2025. Core excluding precious metals is about 235 basis points lower.
- Food prices have fallen since June 2025.
- The IMF puts India's calendar-2025 inflation at 2.8%.
- In December 2025 the RBI cut its FY26 projection from 2.6% to 2.0%. Its FY27 projection is 3.9–4.0% for Q1–Q2; the IMF's is 4.0%.

Quote (para 5.9): "Inflation eased further to 0.3 per cent in October 2025"

FLAG (definition): the IMF's 2.8% is for calendar 2025 and the 1.7% is for April–December 2025. They are not inconsistent.

**ES25-13 — Rate cuts of 125 basis points; CRR down to 3%.** Ch 3, paras 3.10–3.17, pp. 87–90 (st. 138–141); Ch 1, para 1.33, p. 26 (st. 77).
- Repo rate:
  - The MPC cut by 100 basis points over April–December 2025, to 5.25%.
  - Para 1.33 counts 125 basis points since February 2025.
  - The stance moved from accommodative to neutral in June 2025.
- CRR: cut by 100 basis points to 3.0%, in stages over September–November 2025, releasing about ₹2.5 lakh crore.
- Open-market purchases:
  - ₹2.39 lakh crore in April–May 2025;
  - in December, ₹1 lakh crore more plus a USD 5bn forex swap;
  - para 1.33 totals them at ₹6.95 lakh crore from February 2025 to 6 January 2026, plus about $25bn of swaps.
- Bank lending rates (WALR, para 3.17): fresh loans −64 basis points to 8.71%, outstanding loans −56 basis points to 9.21%, over April–November.
- Bad loans (GNPA) are 2.2%.

Quote (para 3.10): "As of December 2025, the repo rate stands at 5.25 per cent."

FLAG (internal):

## Problems and caveats

1. **Rebasing.** ES 2025-26 announces a new GDP base (2022-23) from 27 February 2026 and a new CPI base (2024) from 12 February 2026. Every growth and inflation figure here comes from the old series. Before the book prints FY25 or FY26 numbers as current, check them against the new series.
2. **Large silent revisions.** The ES25 SA raises the 2022-23 and 2023-24 levels a lot. Derived growth goes from 7.0% to 7.6% for 2022-23 and from 8.2% to 9.2% for 2023-24. The main text of ES 2025-26 does not comment on this, at least where I searched. The growth rates are my own calculation from levels; label them as derived if used.
3. **Tariff timeline.** The Preface says the 25% reciprocal tariff was "announced in April". The legal instruments cited (footnote 26, Ch 4) are dated 31 July and 6 August 2025. The Survey never says why the August tariff was imposed. Footnote 26 only gives an Executive Order title referring to the Russian Federation. The book should not explain the penal tariff using this Survey alone.
4. **Period mismatches inside ES 2025-26.** Several figures differ between Chapter 1 and the chapters that follow, mostly because their end-dates differ:
   - the rupee (−6.5% vs −5.4%);
   - lending rates (−59/−69 vs −64/−56 basis points);
   - OMO totals (₹6.95 lakh crore vs ₹2.39 + 1 lakh crore);
   - gross FDI growth (16.1% vs 15.9% derived).

   Always give the period with the figure.
5. **Typos:**
   - para 4.86: "USD 668.3.4 billion";
   - para 3.16: "up to 8 January 2025", which should be 2026.
6. **Extraction.** The two-column layout sometimes joins text across columns. In Ch 1 of ES 2025-26 the page footers are doubled. I checked page offsets at chapter starts and at each quoted page, but para numbers given as "around" (Box III.2, Box II.8) were not pinned exactly.
7. **The ES24 fiscal picture is thin.** ES 2024-25 has no fiscal chapter. Its SA Table 2.4 ends at 2023-24 (PA). For FY25 fiscal figures, rely on ES 2025-26.
8. **The Survey's own claims.** Some statements are the Survey's own judgement, not measurement: "fastest-growing major economy", "punching below its weight", the scenario probabilities, potential growth of 7%. Attribute them to the Survey.
