      How to read it

      • Colours. Each indicator has its own fixed colour classes, set from all its values since 1990, so a country that changes colour between years has moved, not the scale. For indicators that can fall below zero (growth rates, balances, flows), red is below zero, grey is close to zero and blue is above it. For the others, the strongest blue means most: darkest on a light page, brightest on a dark one.
      • Years. Drag the slider, use the arrows, or press Play. Years with data for fewer than a third as many countries as the best-covered year are left off the slider.
      • Countries. Hover for a value; click or tap to see the rank and a link to the country's line in Compare countries. The lists under the map name the ten highest and lowest, and the full list includes countries too small to see.
      • Data. The indicators are the same 47 as in Compare countries, from the IMF World Economic Outlook database (October 2026) and the World Bank World Development Indicators. The page address records the indicator and year.
